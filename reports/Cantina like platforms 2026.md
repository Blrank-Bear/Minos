# New earning platforms in 2026 are few

As of 28 October 2026, very few truly new Cantina-style platforms launched in 2026. The money mostly moved inside platforms that already existed. The new launches with real backing are **CertiK Hunt** (Web3 bounties and contests, invite-only, 1 July 2026), the **OpenAI Safety Bug Bounty** (public, on Bugcrowd, 25 March 2026), **Gray Swan Arena 2.0** with its **$300,000 IPI Black** prompt-injection contest (public, 20 October 2026), **Kaggle Community Hackathons** (March 2026), and **Gensyn's** mainnet and token (April 2026). Many "AI agents earn money" sites also launched in 2026 (AgentOn, Bot Games, agentbounty.org, trybounty.ai), but none of them has payouts that anyone outside the company has confirmed. Older platforms changed more than new ones appeared. **Code4rena announced its wind-down on 13 May 2026**, Immunefi took over its bounty customers, Hats Finance shut down at the end of 2025, and Cantina's bounty pages now redirect to a new domain, cantina.security. Web2 bug bounties got harder for newcomers: public payouts went down and AI-generated report spam pushed programs toward invite-only tiers. For an SN107 Minos miner with ML and config-tuning skills, the best options in 2026 are still contests that score submitted work: other Bittensor submission subnets, forecasting tournaments (Numerai, Allora, CrunchDAO), Kaggle benchmark prizes, and short AI red-teaming contests. Open Web3 audits and agent bounty boards are a worse fit.

**How to read the labels in this report.** **Verified** means an official page, or at least two independent news outlets. **Reported** means one secondary source only. **Claim** means a press release or the company's own numbers. **Observed** means the coordinator of this research checked the live website directly on 28 October 2026.

## Web3 security closed its doors after Code4rena left

The biggest 2026 change in Web3 security is a loss, not a launch. On **13 May 2026 Code4rena said it would wind down**, finishing open contests and engagements. It had been backed by Paradigm and bought by Zellic in 2024. **Immunefi took over Code4rena's bug-bounty customers and researchers** ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/401179)). No source says who takes Code4rena's competitive-audit business; Sherlock and Cantina are the natural winners, but this is not confirmed. Hats Finance had already turned off its hosted service on **31 December 2025** ([ChainCatcher](https://www.chaincatcher.com/en/article/2225329)). Note that Sherlock's August 2026 article still calls Hats "active", which is out of date.

The new entrants are curated, not open. **CertiK Hunt** says openly that it is "intentionally invite-only" and vets both researchers and projects ([CertiK blog](https://www-cn.certik.com/blog/introducing-certik-hunt)). Its first named program, WEMIX, pays up to $10,000 per finding, and the headline calls it an "upgraded" program, so CertiK Hunt is probably a relaunch of an older CertiK bounty brand ([Webull/news wire](https://www.webull.com/news/15744748814672896)). **SpecSiege** caps contests at 50 researchers and **forbids AI-generated submissions**, while CertiK Hunt runs "AI security challenges", so the two disagree about AI-assisted hunting ([Mpost](https://mpost.io/codespect-rolls-out-specsiege-a-curated-audit-contest-platform-for-web3-security/)). Other 2026 launches were AI audit products for customers, not earning platforms: the Sherlock Audit Engine (18 August, sponsored press release) ([Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/375876/sherlock-unveils-audit-engine-as-ai-security-race-accelerates)) and Cyberscope's free Cyberscan AI scanner ([NSE filing](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/corporate/TAC_13052026214546_Cyberscan_PR_India.pdf)).

**Correction about Solana Alpenglow.** The Alpenglow bug bounty did **not** run on Cantina. It was a one-off contest run by **Anza** (the Agave client team) on **Anza's own portal**, from **5 to 19 August 2026**, with a pool of **up to 50,000 SOL** and a **non-refundable 0.5 SOL fee per finding** to stop spam ([anza-xyz/alpenglow on GitHub](https://github.com/anza-xyz/alpenglow), [CryptoSlate](https://cryptoslate.com/solanas-alpenglow-bug-hunt-charges-researchers-0-5-sol-to-report-flaws/)). It got 300+ submissions; payout results were not found ([Crypto Briefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/solana-alpenglow-bug-bounty-closes/)). A fee to submit a bug report is a red flag for researchers, even when the reason is spam control.

**Cantina is still active but is moving its bounty domain.** Directly **observed** on 28 October 2026: `https://cantina.xyz/bounties` returns a **308 permanent redirect to `https://www.cantina.security/bounties`**, while `cantina.xyz/competitions` still serves competitions. No announcement of this move was found, and the research notes (based on search results) had found "no evidence" of cantina.security. The direct check is stronger evidence, so treat the bounty move as real but unannounced. The live competition seen that day was **Revert v4lend: $50,000 USDC, 6 October to 3 November 2026, 3,495 findings submitted, High and Medium severity only, coded proof-of-concept required** (observed). That finding count shows how crowded open contests are: a $50k pool shared among thousands of reports. Cantina also won big 2026 bounties, including a **$1M OKX DEX bounty (30 January)** ([MEXC news](https://www.mexc.com/news/597030)) and a **Paxos bounty up to $1M (March)** ([MEXC news](https://www.mexc.com/news/594955)), and its docs now call it an "agentic security platform" ([Cantina docs](https://docs.cantina.xyz/about-cantina/about-cantina)).

## AI red-teaming is the one area where new public money appeared

AI safety is where 2026 added real, public, well-funded programs. The **OpenAI Safety Bug Bounty** opened publicly on **25 March 2026** on Bugcrowd. It pays for prompt injection, data exfiltration, agents taking disallowed actions, and bugs in connectors and MCP integrations; simple content-policy jailbreaks without real impact do not count ([SecurityWeek](https://www.securityweek.com/openai-launches-bug-bounty-program-for-abuse-and-safety-risks/amp/)). SecurityWeek reports a ceiling of **$7,500**, while Storyboard18 says "up to $100,000" ([Storyboard18](https://storyboard18.com/digital/openai-launches-safety-bug-bounty-program-with-payouts-up-to-100000-93422.htm)); the $100k figure is probably OpenAI's separate security bounty, so $7,500 is the safer number until openai.com confirms it.

**Gray Swan** is the most credible AI red-team marketplace. On **20 October 2026** it relaunched as **Arena 2.0** with **IPI Black**, a **$300,000** indirect-prompt-injection contest sponsored by **Anthropic, Meta, Google DeepMind and Reflection AI**, with new scenarios every week until **2 November 2026** ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/10/20/exclusive-ai-security-company-gray-swan-red-team-arena-contest-to-jailbreak-ai-agents/)). Gray Swan raised a **$40M Series A** in 2026, came out of Carnegie Mellon, and has run arenas since 2024; its co-founder chairs OpenAI's Safety and Security Committee (same source). One research note had marked the $300k contest as unverified because it only saw a Russian-language snippet; the Fortune article settles it. The public arena requires a human to drive each attack and bans automated tools ([Arena rules](https://app.grayswan.ai/arena/about)), so scripting skills help less than creativity here. Gray Swan's best-paid work happens in private, NDA-bound arenas for vetted people.

**Anthropic's model-safety bounty** is not new: it started in August 2024 with rewards up to $15,000 and still needs acceptance before you can submit ([Claude support](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/12119250-model-safety-bug-bounty-program)). A May 2026 Anthropic program paying up to $25,000 appears only on an aggregator and is unverified ([ThreatCluster](https://threatcluster.io/cluster/anthropic-launches-bug-bounty-for-ai-safety-defenses-2de609cc)).

Classic Web2 bug bounty went the other way. AI-written reports flooded programs, and platforms closed doors. HackerOne reportedly **paused Internet Bug Bounty submissions around April 2026**, curl left bug bounties in January, and Google stopped accepting AI-generated reports in March ([Privacy Guides](https://www.privacyguides.org/news/2026/04/17/hackerone-pauses-internet-bug-bounty.md)). From **27 July 2026, GitHub's public tier pays fixed amounts of $250 / $2,000 / $5,000 / $10,000**, lower than before, and moved the higher rewards (critical from about $30,000) into an **invite-only VIP tier** ([TechRadar](https://techradar.com/pro/security/github-restructures-bug-bounty-program-following-flood-of-ai-generated-reports)). Incumbents also launched AI pentest products that compete with human hunters on easy findings: **YesWeHack Agentic Pentest (25 June 2026)** ([YesWeHack](https://www.yeswehack.com/news/yeswehack-agentic-pentest)) and Bugcrowd's Savant Pathseeker in early access ([Bugcrowd](https://www.bugcrowd.com/press-release/bugcrowd-acquires-informer/)). Programs moved between platforms (Adobe to Intigriti from 1 September 2026) ([Adobe](https://blog.adobe.com/security/a-new-home-for-the-adobe-bug-bounty-program)), and regional platforms like Nepal's Bugv added small programs paying roughly $10 to $800 per bug ([Bugv](https://blog.bugv.io/programiz-launched-a-bug-bounty-program/)). No 2026 news was found for Synack or Open Bug Bounty.

## Agent and coding bounty boards launched loudly but pay little

2026 brought many sites that promise income for AI agents or their owners, and this is where red flags are most common. **AgentOn** launched on 8 June with a $100,000 campaign, but its tasks are user growth, content and marketing, and the only source is a press release with no named backers ([GlobeNewswire repost](https://www.techdogs.com/tech-news/globe-newswire/agenton-officially-launches-kicking-off-a-100000-bounty-campaign-for-ai-agents-and-users)). **Bot Games** promised 1 BTC to the best open-source agent from 1 March, but no results or winners were ever found ([PR.com](https://www.pr.com/press-release/960264)). **agentbounty.org** claims "$2.4M total paid" and "8,900+ hunters" but names no operator and shows stale due dates ([agentbounty.org](https://agentbounty.org)); treat it as a possible scam until someone proves a payout.

The serious agent marketplace is **Bounty (trybounty.ai)**, with named founders and $1M from a16z. It had 1,300+ tasks a few weeks after launch, but even its founder only claims that good agents earn "up to $500 per month" ([MENA Startup Digest](https://menastartupdigest.com/?p=46086)). x402-based projects such as **Execution Market**, where agents pay humans USDC for real-world micro-tasks, are early and hackathon-grade ([ETHGlobal](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/execution-market-xc51v)).

Classic GitHub issue bounties did not get a new platform in 2026. **Algora** still leads: contributors keep 100% and are paid when the PR is merged ([Algora pricing](https://console.algora.io/pricing)). But AI agents now crowd it, with about 10 to 20 hunters per bounty and the first valid PR winning ([DEV](https://dev.to/zeroknowledge0x/i-built-an-ai-agent-that-hunts-github-bounties-247-architecture-code-and-brutal-lessons-after-2kma)). Public test reports range from $345 in a week to $0 after three days ([DEV](https://dev.to/hummern/how-ai-agents-earn-345-real-world-bounty-hunting-results-3b3m), [HackerNoon](https://hackernoon.com/i-sent-an-ai-agent-to-hunt-open-source-bounties-for-three-days-it-earned-$0)), and writers warn about fake bounty repos and worthless token payouts. Superteam Earn (Solana) has listings open to agents, but one $3.1k bounty got 479 submissions ([DEV](https://dev.to/neilvolner/i-am-an-ai-agent-given-50-to-make-money-here-is-what-actually-happened-3gjg)).

Steadier technical income moved to **paid AI-training work**. **Toptal ExpertCrowd** launched in October 2026 for RLHF and evaluation work by vetted engineers and claims over $5M in pre-launch revenue ([Toptal](https://www.toptal.com/press-center/toptal-launches-expertcrowd-to-advance-ai-with-world-class-domain-expertise)), and **micro1** is estimated at about $500M annual revenue by August 2026 ([Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/micro1/)). These are vetted jobs, not open contests, and pay rates are not public.

## ML contests and Bittensor still hold the skill-based money

In ML competitions, 2026 added programs, not platforms. **Kaggle Community Hackathons** (March 2026) let anyone host judged hackathons with prizes up to $10,000 ([Gadgetbond](https://gadgetbond.com/kaggle-community-hackathons-launch/)). **ARC Prize 2026** on Kaggle offers **$2M** across ARC-AGI-2 ($700k), ARC-AGI-3 ($850k) and a Paper Prize ($450k), with a Kaggle deadline of **2 November 2026** and open-source code required ([ARC Prize](https://arcprize.org/competitions/2026)). **Numerai** raised **$30M at a $500M valuation**, changed payout rules from 1 January 2026 ([Numerai blog](https://blog.numer.ai/numerai-december-2025-update/)), and launched **Numerai Risk** with a $1M NMR buyback; monthly payouts fell from about $252k (November 2025) to about $182k (January 2026) ([Numerai blog](https://blog.numer.ai/numerai-monthly-numercon-agents-staking-risk-1m-nmr-buyback.md)). **CrunchDAO** now runs on Solana with USDC prize pools held in escrow before contests start ([CrunchDAO FAQ](https://protocol.crunchdao.com/appendix/coordinator-faq.md)) and paid $300k in 2025 ([mlcontests](https://mlcontests.com/state-of-machine-learning-competitions-2025/)). Hosting platforms consolidated: CodaLab moved into Codabench, EvalAI became paid, and Hugging Face Competitions went dormant (same source). CTF prizes stayed small, usually under $15,000 and often non-cash ([CTFtime](https://ctftime.org/event/3252)).

Bittensor is still the most direct match for the reader's skills, but 2026 protocol changes made subnet choice riskier. The **July "Emission Gate"** sends most emission to subnets above a market-set demand line, the **July collateral option** lets owners require deposits to register, and the **October "Etao" change lets owners change the 18/41/41 owner/validator/miner split** ([Grey Area Labs](https://greyarealabs.co/2026/03/23/bittensor-subnets-explained/)). All 128 subnet slots were full on 21 October 2026 (same source). Miner UID registration is a non-refundable burn that can swing 3 to 5 times in a week ([misar.blog](https://www.misar.blog/@synor/articles/bittensor-subnet-mining)). Covenant AI reportedly left Bittensor in April 2026, and SN3 is now "Teutonic" ([Webopedia](https://www.webopedia.com/news/markets/the-top-bittensor-subnets-after-the-covenant-ai-shakeup/)).

## Practical recommendations for an SN107 Minos miner

The reader's advantage is fast, disciplined tuning against a hidden scorer: one change per test, careful logs, and checking results across rounds. That skill pays best where a fixed metric scores submitted work, and worst where speed, luck or marketing decides the winner. The table ranks options by fit, from best to worst.

| Priority | Option | Why it fits | Effort and risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Stay on SN107 Minos | Clear scoring rules; 4.1% emission on $17.5M market cap | Watch Etao (owner can cut miner share) and Emission Gate |
| 2 | Add one similar subnet: SN56 Gradients, SN118 Ditto, SN62 Ridges or SN34 BitMind | Same "submit and get scored" loop | Each needs a UID burn (lost if deregistered); ask before any registration or staking |
| 3 | Forecasting tournaments: Numerai / Numerai Risk, Allora model workers, CrunchDAO | Scored by a fixed metric, CPU-friendly | Token price risk (NMR, ALLO); Allora unlock from Nov 2026 |
| 4 | Kaggle and ARC Prize | Real cash, public, strong reputation value | ARC 2026 deadline is 2 Nov, so plan for 2027 unless already working on it |
| 5 | Gray Swan Arena (IPI Black until 2 Nov, Proving Ground weekly) | Public, no entry cost, top-lab sponsors | Manual work only; prizes go to top ranks |
| 6 | Paid AI-training work (micro1, Toptal ExpertCrowd) | Steady pay for vetted engineers | Application and vetting; pay not public |
| 7 | Web3 audit contests (Cantina, Sherlock, CodeHawks First Flights) | Big pools exist | Steep Solidity learning curve; 3,495 findings for one $50k pool |
| Avoid | agentbounty.org, Bot Games, AgentOn-style quests, token-paid GitHub bounties, contests with submission fees | No proof of payouts or poor fit | High chance of wasted time |

Three rules apply everywhere. First, check the money before the work: look for escrowed prize pools (CrunchDAO, Kaggle), published past payouts, or a named company. Second, prefer published, deterministic scoring; Affine's unpublished rule shows how fast owner discretion can change pay. Third, expect AI tools to raise competition in every open contest; 2026 rewarded people with a track record (invite-only tiers at CertiK Hunt, GitHub VIP, Gray Swan private arenas), so a public record of Minos or Kaggle results is worth building in itself.

## Conclusion

The 2026 market did not create a "new Cantina". It moved from open contests toward curated, invite-only access, mainly because AI-generated submissions made open programs expensive to run. That change cuts both ways for the reader. Open bounty boards and audit contests are now crowded with AI-assisted entrants, which lowers the expected pay for any one person. But programs that score real model quality with hidden tests, such as Minos, Kaggle benchmarks and forecasting tournaments, resist low-effort spam, and they reward exactly the patient, measured tuning the reader already does.

The main open questions are about money flow, not platforms. Nobody has published 2026 payout totals for CertiK Hunt, the OpenAI safety bounty or Gray Swan, and per-miner earnings for Bittensor subnets are not public. The new Bittensor owner powers (Etao, collateral) mean a subnet's terms can change after you invest time. The safest strategy is a small portfolio: one core subnet, one second subnet with similar scoring, and one cash-paying public contest, checked against real payouts every month.
