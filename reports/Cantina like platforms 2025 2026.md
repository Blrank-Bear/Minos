# Few new Cantina rivals launched since 2025

As of 28 October 2026, we found only **five genuinely new Cantina-like platforms** worldwide for 2025–2026. These are places where individual researchers earn money from security contests, bug bounties or crowdsourced testing. **Two launched in 2025**: VulneraPro (Brazil, June 2025) and Bugbop (Australia, alpha April 2025). **Three launched in 2026**: CertiK Hunt (1 July), CODESPECT SpecSiege (about 27 July) and Cyfrin's BattleChain (testnet March, launch state unclear). You are right that 2026 has more than two names if you count wider. Add the AI-agent venue (Sherlock Audit Engine) and the relaunches or self-hosted sites that work like new platforms (Gray Swan Arena 2.0, the Firedancer bounty site, IssueHunt One), and **2026 rises to seven**. Six more 2026 names are possible but unverified (BugSecure Africa, bug.sa, OQDOPPI, BBHunt, BugStream, Kiberbastion). **None of the five confirmed new platforms is a large, open, public venue with verified payouts.** Two are invite-only or capped, two are small regional tools, and one is not clearly live. The reason is simple. In 2025–2026, AI-written reports flooded open programs, investors put their money into AI security startups instead of crowds, and the old open venues shrank (**Code4rena announced its wind-down on 13 May 2026** and **Hats Finance closed on 31 December 2025**). So the market built gates (invitations, KYC, NDAs, submission fees) instead of new open platforms. For an individual today, the only 2025–2026 venues you can join without an invitation are **Gray Swan Arena 2.0**, the **Firedancer bounty site** (1 SOL per report), **Bugbop** and, in practice, **SpecSiege** (apply per contest).

**How to read the labels.** **Verified (V)** means an official page or at least two independent outlets. **Reported (R)** means one secondary source or a search snippet only. **Claim (C)** means a press release, a sponsored article or the company's own numbers. **Observed (O)** means the research coordinator checked the live website on 28 October 2026. Scope covers platforms unveiled in 2025 or 2026. Decentralized AI networks, ML/data competitions, AI-agent task marketplaces, coding bounties and AI-training work are excluded.

The five confirmed platforms show the new pattern. **Only Bugbop is open to anyone**, and it is tiny. **CertiK Hunt and SpecSiege are curated on purpose.** **BattleChain uses the most unusual model.** You do not send a report and wait for triage. You exploit the contract for real under Safe Harbor and keep a share. The platforms also disagree about AI. SpecSiege bans AI-generated submissions, while BattleChain names AI bots as attackers. Other 2025–2026 things that looked new were not counted because they do not pay outside researchers: Hack The Box AI Range (Dec 2025, a benchmark range), Lakera's Gandalf: Agent Breaker (a free game), YesWeHack Agentic Pentest (June 2026, a customer product), the TheDAO Security Fund (January 2026, grants rather than per-finding bounties) ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/387697/the-dao-back-ethereum-dev-griff-green-use-unclaimed-hack-funds-new-ethereum-security/)) and BountyHunt.xyz (a $9/month alert tool) ([Cointelegraph PR](https://cointelegraph.com/press-releases/bountyhuntxyz-launches-to-speed-up-web3-bug-hunting)).

Three forces stand behind these numbers. **First, AI-written reports made open programs expensive to run.** HackerOne reportedly paused Internet Bug Bounty submissions around April 2026, after curl left bug bounties in January ([Privacy Guides](https://www.privacyguides.org/news/2026/04/17/hackerone-pauses-internet-bug-bounty.md)). Immunefi banned AI-generated reports and added fees. Firedancer and Anza charged per report. The new platforms copy this logic: CertiK Hunt is invite-only, SpecSiege caps entry at 50 and bans AI, and Sherlock Audit Engine requires KYC, an NDA and a minimum valid ratio. **Second, investors funded AI replacing crowds, not crowds.** About **$855M** went into 150+ AI-security seed rounds in 2026 ([AIWeekly](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/ai-security-startups-pull-855m-across-150-seed-rounds-in-2026)). Large raises went to Mindgard ($30M) and Armadin ($189.9M) ([Dealroom](https://dealroom.co/news/144657-mindgard-raises-30m-series-a-to-red-team-ai-systems/); [TAMradar](https://www.tamradar.com/funding-rounds/armadin-series-a-189-9m)). The 2025 YC security companies are autonomous hacking tools ([YC](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/industry/cybersecurity)). No funding database showed a new crowdsourced platform. **Third, the market consolidated.** Zellic made Code4rena free in 2025, which made contest platforms hard to run as a business, and then closed it in 2026. Governments put new programs on existing platforms: the EU picked YesWeHack under a framework worth up to €7.68M ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20251001342504/en/YesWeHack-Won-the-European-Commissions-Latest-Bug-Bounty-Tender)), and the Swiss, US and Russian programs also went to existing hosts. The strongest counter-argument is that 2026 created the first paid venues built for human-plus-AI teams (Sherlock Audit Engine, BattleChain, AgentArena). But all three are gated, early or not clearly live, so they do not change the picture.

**Coverage is honest but incomplete.** Seven research tracks ran: Web3 2025, a Web3 2026 deep pass, Web2/mixed, AI red-teaming, an Asian-language sweep, a non-English sweep and a funding/directory sweep. Searches covered **more than 13 languages**: Chinese (simplified and traditional), Japanese, Korean, Vietnamese, Thai, Indonesian, Spanish, Portuguese, French, German, Italian, Polish, Turkish, Russian, Ukrainian, Arabic, Persian and Hebrew. But the **session web-search cap (200) ran out**, and several tracks stopped early. These leads were **never checked**: Procur3 (audit RFP marketplace), Ackee Wake Arena, spin-offs from audit firms (Guardian, OtterSec, Zokyo, SlowMist, Beosin, Verichains, Halborn, Quantstamp), Product Hunt / Reddit / YC 2026 batch listings, Hindi and Thai native sources, Korean exchanges (Upbit, Bithumb), the Chinese Web3 auditors ScaleBit, MoveBit, Salus and Secbit, and the Hyperliquid, Sui and Sonic ecosystems. Platforms marketed only on Telegram, Discord or X would also be missed. "Not found" is weak evidence of absence, especially in Asia, where the search engine indexes local news poorly.

## Conclusion

2025–2026 did not produce a "new Cantina". The real change is **who controls entry**. The confirmed new platforms are either curated extensions of established security firms (CertiK, CODESPECT, Cyfrin) or small regional tools (VulneraPro, Bugbop). The money that grew, in AI red-teaming and government programs, flowed through old hosts such as Gray Swan, Kaggle, Bugcrowd, HackerOne and state platforms. A second change is that **researchers now sometimes pay to play**: Immunefi fees, 1 SOL per Firedancer report and 0.5 SOL for Alpenglow. These fees reward precision and punish volume, including AI-assisted volume.

For an individual, the bottleneck is no longer finding a platform. It is **building a public track record** good enough to pass vetting. Results on open venues now work mainly as a ticket into the invite-only tiers where most new money sits. The biggest unknowns are about money, not names: none of the 2025–2026 platforms has published verified payout totals, and Immunefi has not disclosed its fee.
