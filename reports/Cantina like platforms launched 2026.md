# Only a handful of new Cantina-style platforms launched in 2026

As of 28 October 2026, research found only **six new platforms like Cantina** (places where individual researchers earn money from security contests, bug bounties or red-team contests) that were unveiled in 2026, and **none of them is a fully open, public signup platform with verified payouts**. The six are **CertiK Hunt** (1 July, Web3, invite-only), **CODESPECT SpecSiege** (about 27 July, Web3, capped at 50 researchers), **Sherlock Audit Engine** (public 18 August, Web3, invite-only with KYC, humans and AI auditors compete), **BattleChain by Cyfrin** (testnet March 2026, a blockchain where whitehats legally attack contracts for bounties, launch state unclear), **Gray Swan Arena 2.0** (20 October, AI red-teaming, public, but a relaunch of a 2024 arena), and **bug.sa** (Saudi Arabia, still "coming soon"). Most other 2026 "news" is **new programs on existing platforms**, not new platforms: the OpenAI Safety Bug Bounty on Bugcrowd, the Swiss e-ID bounty going public, LG Uplus on HackerOne, Pakistan's NADRA challenge, India's UIDAI cohort, and Anza's Solana Alpenglow contest on Anza's own portal (not on Cantina). The main reason so few platforms appeared is that **AI-written reports flooded open programs in 2026**, so the market moved toward invite-only, tiered and fee-based access instead of new open platforms. At the same time, the old open venues shrank: **Code4rena announced its wind-down on 13 May 2026** and Hats Finance closed at the end of 2025. One limit applies to the whole report: all searches were in English, so a platform marketed only in Korean, Chinese, Turkish, Arabic, Spanish or another local language could have been missed.

**How to read the labels.** **Verified** means an official page or at least two independent news outlets. **Reported** means one secondary source only. **Claim** means a press release, a sponsored article, or the company's own numbers. **Observed** means the research coordinator checked the live website directly on 28 October 2026.

Three details deserve attention. First, **two of the six are relaunches, not brand-new names**: Gray Swan's arena has run since 2024, and CertiK used the "CertiK Hunt" name before 2026. Second, the platforms disagree about AI. **SpecSiege bans AI-generated submissions**, while **Sherlock Audit Engine and BattleChain invite AI auditors and bots**, and CertiK Hunt runs "AI security challenges". Third, **BattleChain is the most unusual model**: instead of "send a report and wait for triage", the whitehat actually exploits a contract under a Safe Harbor agreement and keeps a share as the bounty. CodeHawks already ran a contest on BattleChain's Confidence Pools code from 9 to 16 July 2026 ([third-party snapshot](https://github.com/munimtechnologies/audit-contest-ai-rules)), but no BattleChain payout has been confirmed.

Not counted as platforms: **AI audit tools for customers** launched in 2026, such as CertiK AI Auditor (April), Cyberscope's free Cyberscan AI (May) ([NSE filing](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/corporate/TAC_13052026214546_Cyberscan_PR_India.pdf)) and YesWeHack Agentic Pentest (25 June) ([YesWeHack](https://www.yeswehack.com/news/yeswehack-agentic-pentest)). These sell testing to companies and do not pay outside researchers. BountyHunt.xyz (June) is a $9/month alert tool, not a platform ([Cointelegraph PR](https://cointelegraph.com/press-releases/bountyhuntxyz-launches-to-speed-up-web3-bug-hunting)).

## AI report floods closed doors instead of opening new platforms

The simplest explanation for so few launches is that **2026 made open programs expensive to run**. AI tools let anyone produce many plausible-looking reports, and triage teams could not keep up. HackerOne reportedly **paused Internet Bug Bounty submissions around April 2026**, curl left bug bounties in January, and Google stopped accepting AI-generated reports in March ([Privacy Guides](https://www.privacyguides.org/news/2026/04/17/hackerone-pauses-internet-bug-bounty.md)). **GitHub split its bounty into two tiers** for reports from 27 July 2026: the public tier now pays fixed amounts of **$250 / $2,000 / $5,000 / $10,000**, and higher rewards (critical from about $30,000) moved to an **invite-only VIP tier** with signal requirements for newcomers ([TechRadar](https://techradar.com/pro/security/github-restructures-bug-bounty-program-following-flood-of-ai-generated-reports)). Web3 platforms wrote AI rules too: Immunefi bans AI-generated reports, HackenProof requires a runnable proof of concept for AI reports, CodeHawks warns that heavy AI use risks disqualification, and Cantina allows AI findings only if validated ([third-party snapshot](https://github.com/munimtechnologies/audit-contest-ai-rules)).

The new platforms follow the same logic. CertiK Hunt explains its invite-only model as a fix for disputes on open platforms, SpecSiege caps entry at 50 and bans AI submissions, and Sherlock Audit Engine requires KYC, an NDA and a minimum valid-finding ratio. A steelman of the opposite view exists: BattleChain and Sherlock Audit Engine treat AI auditors as legitimate competitors, so 2026 also created the first paid venues built for human-plus-AI teams. But both are gated or not yet clearly live, so they do not change the main picture. **The money moved toward people with a track record**, and a new public platform was a poor business bet in this climate.

## Conclusion

2026 did not produce a "new Cantina". It produced **gates**: invitations, KYC, NDAs, valid-ratio thresholds and submission fees. The new platforms that did appear are mostly run by established security firms (CertiK, CODESPECT, Sherlock, Cyfrin) as curated extensions of their own businesses, and the only public one, Gray Swan Arena, is a relaunch. For an individual, this means the real barrier in 2026 is no longer finding a platform; it is **building a public track record** good enough to be invited. Results on open venues (Cantina competitions, CodeHawks First Flights, Gray Swan leaderboards) now work mainly as a ticket into the invite-only tiers where most of the money sits.

The biggest unknowns are about money, not names: no 2026 payout totals exist for CertiK Hunt, Sherlock Audit Engine, BattleChain, SpecSiege or Gray Swan, and Immunefi has not published its submission fee. The English-only search is a real blind spot, especially for China, Korea, Japan, the Middle East and Latin America, and a local-language search is the best next step if wider coverage is needed.
