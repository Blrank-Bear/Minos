# Non-crypto (Web2) open source maintainer/contributor funding launched or opened publicly in 2025–2026

Scope: mainstream programs where individuals/projects get money for maintaining or contributing to OSS. Excludes security bug bounties, crypto, Bittensor, ML competitions. Research date 2026-10-28. "Verified" means a payout or disbursement figure from the operator or independent press; "claimed" means a commitment or announcement only.

## Q1: Which platforms/programs launched or opened publicly in 2025–2026?

### Takeaway
There was no new Web2 "Drips clone" in 2025–26. The new money came from (a) corporate/foundation grant funds (HeroDevs $20M, Rust Foundation Maintainers Fund, GNOME Fellowship, Open Source Endowment), (b) public programs (Sovereign Tech Fellowship pilot 2025 and its expansion in 2026, the EU Open Internet Stack plans), (c) a dependency-based routing product (ecosyste.ms Funds, GA April 2025), and (d) AI-lab compute and credit programs (Anthropic, OpenAI). The closest Web2 analogue to Drips' "fund your dependency tree" model is ecosyste.ms Funds, plus thanks.dev, which is older.

### Cited Findings

**New in 2025–2026 (by launch date)**

- **Open Source Endowment (endowment.dev).** A US 501(c)(3) founded 14 Feb 2025 and launched publicly in late Feb 2026. Donations form a permanent principal and only the investment returns are granted, at about 5%/yr. First grants were slated for Q2 2026 — [FOSS Force](https://fossforce.com/2026/03/can-the-open-source-endowment-fix-what-years-of-neglect-broke/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=3096506); [OpenSourceForU](https://www.opensourceforu.com/2026/03/worlds-first-oss-endowment-backs-maintainers-with-investment-income/)
  - Backers include Thomas Dohmke (former GitHub CEO) and Mitchell Hashimoto, with more than 50 donors at launch — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=3096506)
  - The site currently shows a $765K fund and 120 donors (69 at $1K+). It does not state whether any grants have been paid out — [endowment.dev](https://endowment.dev)
  - Red flag: FOSS Force questions whether a slow-building endowment helps projects that need cash now — [FOSS Force](https://fossforce.com/2026/03/can-the-open-source-endowment-fix-what-years-of-neglect-broke/)
  - Status: claimed. No verified grant payouts were found.
- **ecosyste.ms Funds (funds.ecosyste.ms), run by Open Source Collective and ecosyste.ms.** Announced Dec 2024, generally available 4 Apr 2025, covering 291 ecosystems. Donors pick an ecosystem (language, framework or package), and money is routed to the critical packages in it through each project's chosen funding channel, minus a 10% fee — [ecosyste.ms blog GA](https://blog.ecosyste.ms/2025/04/04/ecosystem-funds-ga.html); [Dec 2024 announcement](https://blog.ecosyste.ms/2024/12/09/ecosystem-funds-curated-support-for-your-critical-software-dependencies.html); [FAQ](https://funds.ecosyste.ms/faq)
  - Verified distribution at GA: a $67,500 commitment from Sentry, and over 80% of funds distributed in 375 payments to 136 projects — [ecosyste.ms blog](https://blog.ecosyste.ms/2025/04/04/ecosystem-funds-ga.html)
  - This is the closest Web2 analogue to Drips' dependency-graph streaming.
- **Sovereign Tech Fellowship (Sovereign Tech Agency, Germany).** A 2025 pilot that paid 6 maintainers for a full year across 18 critical technologies (e.g. tcpdump/libpcap, osm2pgsql). The 2026 round opened to community managers and technical writers, with applications closing 6 Apr 2026, and selected 14 fellows — [sovereign.tech: 2026 applications](https://www.sovereign.tech/news/2026-fellowship-applications-open); [Meet the 2026 fellows](https://www.sovereign.tech/news/meet-the-2026-sovereign-tech-fellows); [osm2pgsql](https://osm2pgsql.org/news/2025/02/06/sovereign-tech-fellowship.html); [Phoronix](https://www.phoronix.com/news/Sovereign-Tech-Fellowship-2026); [heise](https://www.heise.de/en/news/New-fellowship-program-launched-to-promote-open-source-projects-9823040.html?view=print)
  - Status: verified (the fellows were paid and an evaluation was commissioned). Individual stipend amounts were not found.
  - Separately, the Sovereign Tech Agency signs maintenance contracts starting at €50,000, and it has a Sovereign Tech Standards/DIN network that helps maintainers take part in standards work — [Help Net Security](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/07/16/open-source-projects-funding-impact/); [sovereign.tech DIN](https://www.sovereign.tech/news/din-where-standards-and-code-meet)
- **HeroDevs Open Source Sustainability Fund.** Announced 23 Jun 2025: $20M of HeroDevs' own capital, alongside a $125M PSG investment. Grants run from $2,500 to $250,000 for maintainers of widely adopted projects who follow end-of-life (EOL) best practices — [HeroDevs press release](https://www.herodevs.com/blog-posts/herodevs-launches-20-million-sustainability-fund-for-open-source-creators-to-secure-end-of-life-software); [fund page](https://www.herodevs.com/sustainability-fund); [TFIR](https://tfir.io/herodevs-launches-20m-fund-to-support-open-source-maintainers-and-eol-security/)
  - Status: claimed. All figures come from HeroDevs' own announcements, and no independent payout tally was found.
  - Red flag: grants are tied to EOL policies that feed HeroDevs' commercial "Never-Ending Support" business.
- **Rust Foundation Maintainers Fund.** Announced Nov 2025 with no stated size or eligibility at the time. Launched 2 Jun 2026 via RFC #3931, which created a Funding team and a "Maintainer in Residence" program. The first hire was still pending as of June 2026 — [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2025/11/05/rust_foundation_announces_maintainers_fund/); [Rust blog](https://blog.rust-lang.org/2026/06/02/launching-the-rust-foundation-maintainers-fund/); [I Programmer](https://www.i-programmer.info/news/136-open-source/18931-rust-maintainers-fund.html)
  - Status: claimed. Fundraising was still in progress.
- **GNOME Foundation Fellowship (2026).** A new program. The first two recipients (Peter Eisenmann, Sophie Herold) start in July 2026. No amount is stated — [OSS Funded #12](https://buttondown.com/ossfunded/archive/2026-06-15/); [GNOME blog](https://blogs.gnome.org/foundation/?p=20106)
- **Vercel Open Source Program.** Launched Apr 2025 with quarterly cohorts (spring, summer with 28 projects, fall 2025, winter and spring 2026). Members get $3,600 in Vercel credits, not cash — [Vercel spring 2025](https://vercel.com/blog/spring25-oss-program); [summer 2025](https://vercel.com/blog/summer-2025-oss-program); [program](https://vercel.com/open-source-program)
- **AI-lab programs for maintainers (2026, credits/subscriptions rather than cash):**
  - Anthropic "Claude for Open Source" (27 Feb 2026): 6 months of free Claude Max, capped at 10,000 people. Reported eligibility is about 5k+ GitHub stars or 1M+ npm downloads — [The New Stack](https://thenewstack.io/openai-anthropic-open-source/); [Simon Willison](https://simonwillison.net/b/9326)
  - OpenAI "Codex for Open Source" (Mar 2026): 6 months of ChatGPT Pro, Codex Security access and API credits, drawn from a $1M Codex Open Source Fund. One third-party guide says credits go up to $25k per project — [Simon Willison](https://feeds.simonwillison.net/2026/Mar/7/codex-for-open-source/); [OpenAI form](https://openai.com/form/codex-for-oss/); [guide (third-party)](https://codex.danielvaughan.com/2026/03/31/codex-open-source-fund)
- **Linux Foundation $12.5M security grant pool (17 Mar 2026).** Funded by Anthropic, AWS, GitHub, Google, Google DeepMind, Microsoft and OpenAI, and administered by Alpha-Omega and OpenSSF. It targets maintainer capacity against a flood of AI-generated vulnerability reports. Greg Kroah-Hartman said grant money alone won't fix the problem — [LF press release](https://linuxfoundation.org/press/linux-foundation-announces-12.5-million-in-grant-funding-from-leading-organizations-to-advance-open-source-security); [Help Net Security](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/03/17/linux-foundation-12-5-million-grants/)
  - 2026 Alpha-Omega grants include Ruby Central "Security Engineers in Residence" and FreeBSD AI-assisted vulnerability discovery — [Ruby Central](https://rubycentral.org/news/strengthening-security-for-the-ruby-ecosystem/)
- **Public-sector items in 2026 (mostly organisation-level, not individual):**
  - UK Open-Source AI Builder Fund: over £500k in compute plus mentoring — [gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/new-backing-for-open-source-ai-builders-data-centre-design-challenge-and-robotics-partnership)
  - NSF "Pathways to Enable Secure Open-Source Ecosystems": up to $40M — [NSF](https://www.nsf.gov/tip/updates/nsf-investing-secure-open-source-ecosystems) (both listed via [OSS Funded #12](https://buttondown.com/ossfunded/archive/2026-06-15/))
- **Small startups.** BountyHub (GitHub issue bounties) launched on Product Hunt 8 Jun 2025, with a small vote count. The only source is a startup directory — [Innolope](https://innolope.com/pulse/startups/6915ab49f915b3ab4c71cbdd). Unverified, with minimal traction.

**Older programs (pre-2025) with their 2025–26 changes**

- **FLOSS/fund (Zerodha, India; launched Oct 2024).** It gives $1M/yr in grants of $10k–$100k and applicants file a `funding.json` manifest — [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2024/10/17/zerodha_open_source_fund/)
  - Tranche 1 (30 May 2025) was $325k to 9 projects — [floss.fund](https://floss.fund/)
  - Tranche 2 (Oct 2025) was $675k to about 30 projects, e.g. FFmpeg $100k (pending), F-Droid $50k, Blender, KDE, Matrix and Wireshark $25k each — [It's FOSS](https://itsfoss.com/news/ffmpeg-receives-100k-funding/); [The DropTimes](https://www.thedroptimes.com/node/55345)
  - Red flag: only about $195k had actually been disbursed by Oct 2025 because of cross-border payment problems — [The DropTimes](https://www.thedroptimes.com/node/55345)
- **GitHub Secure Open Source Fund (announced Nov 2024).** Each project gets $10k via GitHub Sponsors ($6k during the sprint, then $2k at each of the 6- and 12-month check-ins), plus a 3-week security program — [GitHub blog Aug 2025](https://github.blog/2025-08-11-securing-the-supply-chain-at-scale-starting-with-71-important-open-source-projects/)
  - By Aug 2026 it had paid $1.88M across 188 projects (290 maintainers in 42 countries). Session 4 had 50 projects and over $500k, and Session 5 applications closed 24 Aug 2026 — [GitHub blog Aug 2026](https://github.blog/open-source/maintainers/what-50-open-source-projects-taught-us-about-security-in-the-ai-era/)
  - Only maintainers in regions supported by GitHub Sponsors can take part — [SD Times](https://sdtimes.com/security/github-launches-new-funding-program-to-improve-security-of-open-source-projects/)
- **GitHub Sponsors.** Passed $100M paid out (Jul 2026), with about 70k maintainers and orgs and over 280k sponsors in 103 regions. Growth is attributed to invoice billing, bulk sponsorships and wider regional coverage. The figures are self-reported and not audited — [IT Brief](https://itbrief.com.au/story/github-sponsors-tops-usd-100-million-for-open-source); [Help Net Security](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/07/21/open-source-github-sponsors-100-million/); [lenz.io fact-check](https://lenz.io/c/github-sponsors-100-million-invested-open-source-f93c1df2)
- **Open Source Pledge (launched Oct 2024).** Companies commit $2,000 per developer per year. Mid-2025: 34 companies had paid $2.65M — [GitHub Maintainer Month](https://maintainermonth.github.com/schedule/2025-05-06-OS-Pledge)
  - By its second anniversary: 37 members had paid $7,421,535 cumulatively — [Sentry blog](https://blog.sentry.io/another-year-another-1-000-000-to-open-source-maintainers/)
  - Sentry gave about $750k in 2025 — [Sentry](https://blog.sentry.io/another-year-another-750-000-to-open-source-maintainers)
  - Red flag: the figures are self-reported by members through blog posts.
- **thanks.dev (launched 2023).** Scans a company's dependencies three levels deep and splits payments across them — [The Register 2023](https://www.theregister.com/2023/04/07/thanksdev_open_source_funding/); [FOSDEM 2025](https://fosdem.org/2025/schedule/event/fosdem-2025-4149-funding-open-source-projects-through-dependency-analysis)
  - Canonical committed $120k over 12 months ($10k/month), reaching over 350 projects by May 2025 — [OMG! Ubuntu](https://www.omgubuntu.co.uk/2025/05/canonical-commits-funds-open-source-devs); [Canonical](https://canonical.com/blog/canonical-thanks-dev-giving-back-to-open-source-developers)
  - Sentry routed $495k through thanks.dev — [Sentry](https://blog.sentry.io/another-year-another-1-000-000-to-open-source-maintainers/)
  - The fee is reported both as a 5% commission and as a voluntary tip, so sources conflict.
- **Tidelift.** Acquired by Sonar (deal announced late 2024). Sonar promised no immediate changes and continues to pay "lifters" based on usage and the strategic importance of their packages — [Sonar PR](https://sonarsource.com/company/press-releases/sonar-to-acquire-tidelift); [Sonar maintainers page](https://www.sonarsource.com/open-source-maintainers)
- **NLnet / NGI Zero (EU).** Commons Fund calls ran through 2026, with the 12th call Feb–Apr 2026 and the 13th Apr–1 Jun 2026, offering €5k–50k grants — [Cyprus funding portal](https://www.fundingprogrammesportal.gov.cy/en/call/ngi-zero-commons-fund-13th-open-call-reclaim-the-public-nature-of-the-internet-en-2026/)
  - On 12 Jun 2026 NLnet paused most open calls while it reviews NGI (1,215 NGI Zero projects funded from about 8,500 applications). It plans three new "Open Internet Stack" programmes (Restack, CodeSupply, ELFA) after the summer. Only NGI Taler and NGI Fediversity stay open — [NLnet](https://nlnet.nl/news/2026/20260612-NGIZero-stocktaking.html)
- **EU Sovereign Tech Fund.** Still only a proposal. The civil-society feasibility study (Jul 2025) asks for at least €350M over 7 years in the 2028–34 MFF. The Commission's 2026 tech-sovereignty package includes an open source strategy, but there is no fund yet — [OpenFuture](https://openfuture.eu/blog/public-letter-calls-for-eu-investment-in-open-source-sustainability-through-an-eu-sovereign-tech-fund/); [TechPolicy.Press](https://techpolicy.press/how-the-eus-tech-sovereignty-package-finally-puts-open-source-to-the-test)
- **Outreachy.** Funding shrank. The Dec 2025 cohort moved to a model where communities fund the internships, and the May 2026 cohort ran with reduced funding and activities because corporate donations fell — [Outreachy Dec 2025 call](https://www.outreachy.org/blog/2025-08-14/december-2025-call-for-mentoring-organizations/); [May 2026 call](https://www.outreachy.org/blog/2026-02-06/may-2026-call-for-mentoring-organizations/)
- **Open Collective.** opencollective.com is being reworked into a general fiscal host for small groups under the temporary "Common-Unity Fund" branding (Jul 2025) — [Open Collective update](https://opencollective.com/commonunity/updates/open-collective-refresh)
- **Polar.sh.** Relaunched in 2024 (v1.0, $1.8M pre-seed) as a broader monetization platform. Issue funding is still documented, but no 2025–26 change could be verified — [Polar blog](https://blog.polar.sh/polar-v1-0-lets-fix-open-source-funding/); [docs](https://docs.polar.sh/docs/issue-funding)
- **Foundation fundraising (not new platforms).** The Django Software Foundation raised its annual goal to $500k (Jun 2026) to fund its Fellows. The PHP Foundation has a target of $700k+/yr — [OSS Funded #12](https://buttondown.com/ossfunded/archive/2026-06-15/)

### Inferences
- In 2025–26, money moved toward (1) curated grant funds run by companies or foundations, (2) "fellowship / maintainer-in-residence" salaried roles (Sovereign Tech Agency, Rust, GNOME, Django), and (3) security-framed money from AI labs. Open permissionless "stream to your deps" platforms did not grow in the same way.
- For an individual looking to earn, the most accessible options are GitHub Sponsors (and SOSF on top of it), FLOSS/fund (apply with funding.json), HeroDevs grants (if you have an adopted project and an EOL policy), the Sovereign Tech Fellowship (competitive, about 14 places/yr) and NGI Zero (paused until its programmes restart after summer 2026).
- Verified payout data is strongest for GitHub SOSF ($1.88M), FLOSS/fund (tranches announced, but disbursement lagging), ecosyste.ms Funds (375 payments) and the Open Source Pledge (self-reported). It is weakest for HeroDevs and the Open Source Endowment.
- The common red flag is disbursement lag and self-reported totals. Several 2026 AI-lab programs give credits rather than cash.

### Gaps
- No 2025–26 national OSS funds were found for France, Japan, India (government) or Switzerland. Search results only surfaced Germany. The UK item is about AI compute, not maintainers.
- No evidence was found on Microsoft FOSS Fund or Google OSS program changes in 2025–26, apart from Google sponsoring Open VSX.
- No 2025–26 changes were verified for Polar, Tidelift's lifter payments, GSoC 2026 or LFX Mentorship stipends.
- Payout totals for HeroDevs' fund, the Open Source Endowment's first grants, and Sovereign Tech Fellowship stipend amounts were not found.
- No successors to the Open Collective Foundation shutdown (2024) were researched in detail.
- Search turned up no new venture-backed "pay your dependencies" startup in 2025–26 that rivals thanks.dev. That absence may reflect search limits rather than a real absence.
